Before you begin

This diagnostic is designed to identify your current strengths, areas for refinement and opportunities to improve scientific precision before your December 9 unit test.

52 core questions + 8 optional preview questions. Aim for about 90 minutes for the core assessment; the preview may take another 15 minutes. You may take a short break and resume. Accuracy matters more than speed.

Assessment expectations

  1. Complete the assessment independently. Do not consult notes, online searches, AI tools or other people.
  2. Use the built-in calculator, reference panel and scrap paper. Enter your reasoning where requested.
  3. Choose an honest confidence rating. If a concept has not yet been taught, indicate that rather than looking it up.
  4. Drafts are automatically saved and may be revisited. Submitted answers are locked; no answer feedback is shown during the assessment.
  5. The eight preview questions on acids, bases and pH are not included in the core score. Titrations are excluded.

Time management and pacing

The timer is a learning tool, not a punitive measure. Each question has a suggested completion time to help you allocate time according to difficulty and mark value. You will not lose marks, be forced to submit, or be prevented from finishing if you exceed the suggested time.

If you are making meaningful progress, continue. If you are stuck, flag the question and return later; your unfinished work is preserved. Secure the questions you can answer, revisit challenging ones, then check calculations, units, significant figures and scientific explanations before submitting. The timer can be hidden or paused for a break.

Solubility rules (usual aqueous conditions)
Group 1, ammonium and nitrates: soluble. Chlorides, bromides and iodides: generally soluble except Ag⁺, Pb²⁺ and Hg₂²⁺. Sulfates: generally soluble; Ba²⁺, Pb²⁺, Sr²⁺ are common exceptions; CaSO₄ is sparingly soluble. Carbonates and phosphates: generally insoluble except group 1 and ammonium. Hydroxides: mostly insoluble except group 1; Ca, Sr and Ba hydroxides are more soluble.

Unit equivalences
1 L = 1000 mL; 1 kg = 1000 g.
